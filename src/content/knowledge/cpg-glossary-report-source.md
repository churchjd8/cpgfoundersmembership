# CPG Glossary — Research Source

**Audience:** First-time U.S. CPG founders

**Date:** September 3, 2026

**Deliverable:** Interactive glossary at `/cpg-glossary`

## Scope and assumptions

The glossary covers the working vocabulary founders encounter across retail, channels, sales, distribution, syndicated data, finance, operations, packaging, ecommerce, fundraising, food safety, and U.S. regulation. It is educational, not legal, accounting, regulatory, investment, or food-safety advice. Customer contracts and data-provider definitions can differ.

## Direct answer

A natural channel broker is a manufacturer's representative specializing in natural, organic, wellness, and specialty retail. The broker brings buyer relationships, category knowledge, account administration, promotional management, and retailer-specific context. The broker does not warehouse and deliver inventory like a distributor and does not replace the founder's responsibility for the sell story, buyer relationship, execution, or velocity.

## Editorial synthesis

Jeff Church's material is the editorial backbone. His recurring operating distinctions shape the definitions and founder notes: broker versus distributor; authorization versus availability; sell-in versus sell-through; distribution versus velocity; gross sales versus net revenue; and gross margin versus contribution margin. External sources are used to verify regulatory language, standardized identifiers, syndicated-data measures, and securities terminology.

The published page contains 331 unique terms across nine operating areas. Entries are searchable by term, expansion, alias, definition, and founder note. Direct fragment links make individual answers shareable. The ecommerce and digital-marketing section includes acquisition, attribution, media efficiency, conversion, retention, repeat-purchase, and customer-value terminology. Jeff's 304-term `CPG_Founders_Dictionary.md` was subsequently reconciled with the original web dataset: overlapping concepts were merged, while gaps in trade spend, channels, retail compliance, manufacturing, Amazon, fundraising, data, and the CPG ecosystem were added.

## Material limitations

- Retailer, distributor, and data-provider terminology is not perfectly standardized.
- Accounting classifications vary; each metric should state what costs and revenue adjustments it includes.
- U.S. legal and regulatory definitions can change and may depend on product, facility, claim, offering, or exemption.
- The glossary favors broadly useful founder vocabulary rather than technical completeness within any single professional specialty.
