July 3, 2018. Five o'clock in the afternoon.

The house was full of family, everybody prepping for the Fourth. And then my phone rang.

Scott Uzzell, president of Coca-Cola's Venture & Emerging Brands division, was on the other end. My heart pounded when I heard his voice. Then came the verdict.

Coca-Cola was not moving forward with the acquisition.

I hung up. Walked downstairs. And in front of my boys, I lost it completely.

I wasn't thinking about the sixteen percent revenue growth we'd posted in the prior twelve months, or the awards, or the growth story. I was thinking: *how did I let us get to this place?*

We had $40 million in secured debt coming due in October. We were burning over $10 million a year. Gross margins were sitting below 32 percent. The strategic partner who was supposed to be our next chapter had just walked away.

The next morning: fireworks, a parade, blue skies, eighty-two degrees. Not a cloud in sight. And I felt sick to my stomach.

Here's what that moment taught me. And it's the thing nobody really tells you when they talk about CPG turnarounds.

The decision that saves you is almost never the one you make *on the day* you need saving. It's usually one you made eighteen months earlier. On a Tuesday. When nobody was watching.

---

**The Product Nobody Was Paying Attention To**

Back in 2014, our head of sales, Nicky Vidaurreta, started pitching two-ounce wellness shots to every buyer she could get in front of. At the time, Suja was the fastest-growing cold-pressed juice brand in the country. We'd gone from zero to nearly $70 million in three years. We had more pressing problems.

The shots were a side project. Bryan Riblett, our head of culinary and formulations, was experimenting in the background. Nobody was really focused on them. They were small, niche, and honestly a long way from the flagship 16-oz green juices that had built our reputation.

Meanwhile, Linda went to see a fortune teller named Tara in 2014. Usually it was about the kids. This time, Tara predicted that the product that would ultimately create the most value for Suja would be wellness shots. We laughed about it afterward. We barely had any shots to speak of yet.

As it turns out, the fortune teller was right.

Four years later, in the summer of 2018, while we were grinding through the hardest season in the company's history, Nicky secured a critical win: she swapped our kombucha shelf space at Costco into the wellness shots line.

That one trade changed everything.

Kombucha was outsourced. Gross margin: about 12 percent. Wellness shots were produced internally. Gross margin: roughly 60 percent. Moving that shelf space moved our company-wide gross margin from approximately 32 percent to nearly 40 percent. One product mix shift. That was the difference between surviving and getting sold in a fire sale.

Revenue is exciting. Gross margin is transformational. I mean that literally.

**The 18-Month Window You Might Be Ignoring**

Here's what I teach every founder I work with now.

Your most important product decision today is the one that will matter eighteen months from now.

Not the SKU that's already performing. Not the line extension a buyer requested last week. The experiment. The side project. The thing that feels a little too early and not quite ready and a bit uncertain.

That's the Tuesday decision.

Because when the crisis comes, and at some point in CPG it will come, you will not have time to build something new from scratch. You'll need to reach into your portfolio and find the thing that's already close. The thing someone on your team believed in before anyone else did.

If it's not there, you're improvising under fire. That rarely ends well.

There's a quote from the book I've been working on that sums it up. I want you to sit with this one:

*"The decision that saves you is almost never the one you made on the day you needed saving. It's usually one you made eighteen months earlier, on a Tuesday, when nobody was watching."*

That's not philosophy. That's the Suja story. That's the wellness shot story. And I've seen it play out at company after company.

**Plan B in Parallel**

I learned to build what I call "Plan B in Parallel."

When launching a new product line, I almost always develop an alternative alongside it. A backup that's quietly percolating. At least half the time, Plan A doesn't materialize the way you expected. Markets shift. Retailers push back. The formula doesn't land with consumers the way you thought.

Plan B preserves your options. It keeps retailer confidence intact. It buys you time for awareness, trial, and repeat to build out. And every once in a while, Plan B becomes the company.

Look at the Trader Joe's celery juice story. 2019. Celery juice exploded after a boost on *The Rachael Ray Show*. TJ's reached out. Their normal preference is private label, but that would have taken too long through their usual process. Because Suja had manufacturing flexibility and a real relationship with TJ's, we went from the first phone call to product on shelves nationwide in under six weeks. Coca-Cola's Stage-Gate process would have required sixteen to eighteen months to do the same thing.

Speed matters. But speed only matters if you have the portfolio ready to deploy.

---

**The Question to Ask Yourself Today**

Look at your product portfolio right now. Not what's selling best. Not what a buyer asked for.

Ask yourself: what is my wellness shot?

What product or initiative are you developing right now, with modest resources and modest expectations, that could change your margin profile or your distribution story in eighteen months? If you don't have an answer, you've got a gap worth closing.

Here's the tactical framework I actually use:

**Map your gross margin by SKU, right now.** You need at least 40 percent company-wide by end of year two. Closer to 50 percent by years three or four. If you have products running at 12 percent gross margin, those are your kombucha. They're not necessarily bad products. But they're pulling the whole business down while you're trying to build something durable. "Gross margin determines destiny" is not a bumper sticker. It's an operating reality.

**For every major product line you launch, build an alternative in parallel.** Not a vague fallback plan. An actual formulation in development. A second retailer relationship. A different pack size or format you could move to quickly. Something real. Plan B doesn't help you if it only exists in a slide deck.

**Listen to your Nicky.** Most founder teams have someone quietly pushing an idea that feels too early. A product that doesn't fit the current narrative. A margin opportunity nobody's fully focused on yet. That person is often more right than you think. Give them some runway. Not a full budget. Just enough to keep it alive and moving.

The shots were Nicky's idea. For years. And the whole company is different because of it.

**The One That Gets You**

I've raised $212 million over forty-four rounds across eight companies. I've seen every kind of pressure this industry generates. And I can tell you with real certainty: the companies that get into the deepest trouble aren't usually the ones hit by something nobody saw coming.

They're the ones that saw it coming and didn't prepare.

The gross margin that was always too thin but felt impossible to fix. The product line everyone knew wasn't working but kept funding because the alternative felt uncertain. The innovation that needed eighteen months of quiet development but never got it.

Hope is not a strategy. And it is absolutely not a forecasting model.

You have an eighteen-month window right now. Every founder does. The question is whether you're doing anything with it.

Tara the fortune teller knew about the wellness shots in 2014. They saved us in 2018. Four years. That's the gap between the Tuesday decision and the payoff. That's the game.

Start making your Tuesday decision today.
