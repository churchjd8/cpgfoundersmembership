Read this before you trust the model

The one that still matters

  • Jeff’s own draw is not in this P&L. JC comp is Joshua’s $10K fee. Every net number on this page is what the business throws off before Jeff pays himself a dollar. Decide what he takes and the real margin drops by that much.
  • There is no $997 MBA product in Stripe. Not a pricing question — the thing does not exist as a sellable object. Every dollar in “book funnel & digital” depends on building it before January. Until then that row is a plan, not revenue.
  • Babu has never been sold, only signed up for. 4, 5, and 4 signups in June, July, August — all organic, no acquisition spend behind them. The model grows from that rate, not from a target.
  • Jake’s first month is outside this window. He starts in August and runs seven months, so six land in Sep–Feb and $4,250 sits in August where this model cannot see it.

What the model does not include

  • Existing container clients are seeded as billing through September and converting in October. Actual start dates vary by a few weeks.
  • Payment processing is applied to all revenue including the off-platform Supermush retainer, which slightly overstates fees.
  • No taxes, no legal or accounting, no travel, no insurance.
  • Book royalties and retail/wholesale copies are excluded — only the free-plus-shipping funnel is modeled.
  • Client capacity is capped by the slider, but Jeff’s actual delivery ceiling across containers plus continuity plus the monthly group call is the real constraint.
  • Webinar operations carry no cost line because Joshua runs them — that labor is inside the $10K comp, not on top of it.
  • Acquisition ramps in over the first few months rather than starting at full speed. September closes almost nothing; the machine is still being built.

Where the leverage actually is

  • Continuity churn. Move it from 5% to 3% and the steady-state pool nearly doubles. Nothing else in this model has that slope. Five $1,500 clients cancelled between February and June.
  • Continuity conversion. Every container graduate who doesn’t roll into $1,500/mo is roughly $18,000 of annual revenue walking out.
  • Babu paid users. Below break-even it is a fixed drag on every other line. Above it, it is the highest-margin thing in the business.
  • Registrant volume matters least of the four. Pull the retention levers first.
