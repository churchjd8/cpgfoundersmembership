# LinkedIn Post — Tuesday, May 6
**Pillar: Personal / Vulnerability**

---

I want to tell you about the worst thing that ever happened to me in business.

Not because it's a fun story. Because it might save you.

Early in my career, I was building a company called Universal Building Products. We'd just closed a $150 million acquisition. It was a strong market with a solid thesis and experienced partners. On paper, everything checked out.

Within weeks of closing, a tragic workplace accident claimed a life. Federal investigations shut us down. Litigation mounted. We were carrying heavy debt just as the market collapsed. I was still learning my way around the building as CEO and now had to navigate this tragedy while the whole thing came apart underneath me.

The company went bankrupt.

That experience rebuilt how I operate. Entirely. It taught me three things I carry to this day:

1. Due diligence is non-negotiable. Not the kind where you skim the deck and trust your gut. The kind where you turn over every rock, even the ones nobody wants to look under.
2. Capital buffers aren't optional. If your plan only works when everything goes right, you don't have a plan. You have a prayer.
3. Real leadership is measured by how you carry people through crisis, not by the wins. Anyone can lead when things are working. The moments that define you are the ones where everything is falling apart and people are looking at you for answers you don't have yet.

Most "founder content" online is highlight reels. Especially as I get older and later in my career, I'm not interested in that.

The lessons that actually protect you come from the painful stuff. The stuff most people won't talk about.

I've had 5 home runs and 3 strikeouts across 8 companies. I talk about both, because pretending the strikeouts didn't happen doesn't help anyone.

If that sounds useful, I'm sharing more of these lessons here regularly. Be sure to follow along!
