# What's Working on Jeff's Account — And How to Run the Same Play for Mark

A field guide, not a theory. Everything below comes from the actual edits that moved Jeff's posts from "fine" to "this sounds like a real person who's lived it." The goal here: bottle those principles so we can point them at Mark Rampolla's content without losing what makes Mark, Mark.

---

## The One-Line Version

**The posts that perform are the ones that read like a voice note from a battle-tested operator, not a polished LinkedIn "thought leader" deck.** Warmth over drama. Story over thesis. Rough over slick. A real human who was actually in the room.

Your three instincts are exactly right, and they're the backbone of this doc:
1. Start inside a story, in the moment.
2. Leave it a little rough around the edges.
3. Make it feel real, personal, human-written — never AI-written.

---

## The 3 Pillars (Your Instincts, Backed by the Edits)

### Pillar 1 — Start inside a story, in the moment

The single highest-leverage move. Don't open with a thesis, a dateline, or a "here's a truth" hook. Drop the reader into a specific scene where something is happening.

**What loses (the AI default):**
- "October 2012. Two months after we launched Suja." (dramatic dateline)
- "Everyone wants to talk about marketing. Almost nobody wants to talk about trade spend." (contrarian cold open)
- "Here's the truth nobody tells you..."

**What wins (the edit):**
- "I want to tell you about one of the not-smart moves I made in October 2012..."
- "True story that most people don't know (but I got nothing to lose at this point, so here you go!)"
- "Okay folks, let's talk about trade spend for a moment."

The difference: a **narrative invitation**, not a headline. You're pulling someone into a story you're about to tell them, the way you'd start it across a table.

> **For Mark:** This is already baked into his voice profile — his LinkedIn should "always open with a specific personal scene or moment." His version of the in-the-moment open is sensory and located: *"I was sitting in the Rancho when..."* / *"We met in front of my old house, Marcus stepping out of his black Porsche Cayenne..."* Where Jeff opens with a warm conversational invite, **Mark opens by putting you in the physical room** — the weather, who's there, what was said. Same principle (start in the moment), different texture (Jeff = host welcoming you in; Mark = cinematic scene-set).

---

### Pillar 2 — Leave it rough around the edges

Polish reads as corporate. Corporate reads as fake. Fake reads as AI. The imperfection **is** the credibility.

What "rough" actually means in the Jeff edits:
- **Flowing, connected sentences over clean staccato fragments.** My instinct is to split for punch ("Same demo cost. 3x the gain. AND political capital."). The edit connects them ("Same demo cost but 3x the gain. AND political capital to expand."). Conversational rhythm, not poetry.
- **Conspiratorial asides and parentheticals** that a copywriter would cut: "(but I got nothing to lose at this point, so here you go!)" / "Even my kids questioned it (rightfully so)."
- **Natural filler is a feature, not a bug:** "honestly," "actually," "rightfully so," "I'll never forget the moment." These are the sound of a person talking. Don't scrub them.
- **Kill the didactic wrap-up.** The AI instinct is to end with "Here's the lesson: [bullet points]." The edit cuts it entirely and lets the story land. Trust the reader. The story IS the lesson.

> **For Mark:** Same principle, dialed differently. Mark's roughness shows up as **real-memory texture, not clean copy.** When we hand him a punchy board-member quote ("You're the problem you keep trying to solve"), he swaps it for how someone actually talks: "Mark, when you're hot, you're on fire... But you run hot and cold and the business needs you to be hot or warm, not cold." He edits for **pacing over completeness** — cutting specifics that don't serve the story ("Scaling ZICO. Building GroundForce. Buying ZICO back" → "I went through it twice"). The lesson: rough = human recall, not tidy narrative.

### Pillar 3 — Real, personal, human — never AI

The tells that scream "a machine wrote this," and the antidotes.

| AI tell | The fix |
|---|---|
| Over-dramatized, punchy-for-punchy's-sake | Would he *actually* raise his voice here if speaking aloud? If not, plain text. |
| ALL CAPS everywhere (4-6 words/post) | 1-3 words max. Only the single most-loaded word. |
| Double exclamation marks `!!` as default emphasis | Single `!`. Reserve `!!` for the one highest-energy moment. |
| Contrarian "wake up sheeple" framing | Mentor framing: "Here's what I tell every founder I work with..." |
| Corporate `we` in a personal story | First-person `I` for personal decisions. `We` only for company actions. |
| Binary either/or framing ("X isn't the game, Y is!!") | Both/and: "X is only part of the game. Y is the real game." |
| Gen-Z / internet vocab ("vibes," "lowkey," "based") | Operator vocabulary. These are grown, credentialed founders, not posters. |
| A tidy summarizing conclusion | Let it end on the story or a single quotable line. Forward motion, not a bow. |

The meta-rule from all of it: **less prophet-on-the-mountain, more senior operator who's seen everything and is willing to share.**

> **For Mark:** The AI-tell fixes transfer almost 1:1, with two Mark-specific notes:
> - **Mark uses *fewer* exclamation points than Jeff, not more.** His energy comes from the substance of the story, not punctuation. No emoji, no hyped language. Where Jeff will drop a single `!`, Mark stays level.
> - **Mark names the ego payoff, not just the struggle.** A huge authenticity move in his edits: he admits the dysfunction *felt good.* "I also got to feel like the hero. That feels great but has a price." / "I wore it like a badge of honor. The busier I was, the more important I felt." That self-aware honesty is his version of "human, not AI."

## Where Jeff and Mark Diverge (Don't Copy-Paste the Voice)

Same **principles**, different **dials**. If you run Jeff's exact settings on Mark, it'll feel off. Quick translation table:

| Dimension | Jeff Church | Mark Rampolla |
|---|---|---|
| Persona | Battle-scarred CPG operator ("Babu"/grandpa energy) | Reflective founder-philosopher; slightly-older friend who made the mistakes first |
| Story open | Warm conversational invite ("I want to tell you about...") | Cinematic sensory scene ("I was sitting in the Rancho...") |
| Emphasis tools | Single `!`, sparse ALL CAPS, occasional profanity | Almost no `!`, no caps, no profanity — energy from substance |
| Signature device | Aphorisms ("Gross margin determines destiny") | Reversals ("Freedom isn't the finish line; it's the starting line") + "Not X but Y" reframes |
| Close | Land on the story or a punchy truth. Warm, generous CTA. | **Coaching close** — turn the mirror on the reader with a feeling-invitation or challenge question ("How might this be true?" / "Are you?") |
| Vulnerability | Quick, sharp, a parenthetical admission | Deeper, names the emotional payoff and the cost |
| Branded language | Numbered frameworks (8-Step, 18 Fatal Flaws) | Tools woven in organically (Zone of Genius, Full Body Yes, the inner playbook) — never as a list |

**The biggest single difference:** Jeff's posts *land and stop.* Mark's posts *land, then turn the mirror back on the reader.* Mark's closings are required coaching moments — reflective questions, "feel everything that comes up," or a direct challenge. Don't end a Mark post the way you'd end a Jeff post.

## The Draft-to-Post Checklist (Run This on Every Mark Draft)

Adapted from the Jeff editing passes that consistently pull a draft toward "real":

1. **Open in a scene.** Is the first line a specific moment with a place, a person, a physical detail? If it's a thesis or a dateline, rewrite it as a scene.
2. **Connect the fragments.** Reconnect ~30% of punchy fragments into flowing sentences with "but/and/so." Keep fragments only for genuine punch moments.
3. **Cut the caps and bangs.** ALL CAPS down to 1-3 words. For Mark, near-zero exclamation points.
4. **Kill the wrap-up.** Delete any "Here's the lesson:" summary block. Trust the story.
5. **Reframe any contrarian hook** into mentor/peer framing.
6. **Scan for AI vocab.** No "vibes," no "10X," no "crush it," no buzzwords.
7. **Add one vulnerable micro-moment** — and for Mark, name the ego payoff (how the dysfunction felt good).
8. **Mark-only: add the coaching close.** End with a reflective question or feeling-invitation that turns the mirror on the reader.
9. **Read it out loud.** If it doesn't sound like Mark talking to a friend over a long dinner, it's not done.

## The North Star

For both men, the target is the same: **it should read like a transcribed voice note from someone who was actually there and has nothing to prove.** Jeff proves it with operator scars and blunt aphorisms. Mark proves it with sensory scenes and hard-won self-awareness. The principles are shared. The voice is not. Write the principle, dial the voice.
