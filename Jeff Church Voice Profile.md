## Voice Profile — Jeff Church

Derived from full analysis of:
1. "The Art of the Startup: A Founder's Playbook for Building and Scaling a $100 Million CPG Brand" (198 pages, ~80,000 words)
2. "Fatal Flaws for Early-Stage Emerging CPG Brands" whitepaper
3. "Lessons Learned Through Growing a Brand to $100 Million in 5 Years" (Suja) whitepaper
4. 27+ blog posts published on cpgfoundersgroup.com (through April 2026)
5. Email nurture sequences, resource delivery emails, and LinkedIn posts

The book represents Jeff's polished, long-form voice. The whitepapers represent his raw, unfiltered, rapid-fire voice. The blog posts show his evolved teaching voice — structured frameworks backed by specific stories. LinkedIn and emails show his most casual, direct register.

---

### TONE & REGISTER

- **Conversational authority** — writes like he's talking to you across a table, but with real weight and credibility behind every word. Not a professor lecturing — a battle-scarred founder sharing war stories over coffee.
- **Warm but direct** — never preachy, never soft. Tells hard truths with empathy. Won't sugarcoat, but also won't be harsh for the sake of it. The whitepapers and LinkedIn posts show he gets MORE direct and blunt in shorter formats.
- **Confident without arrogance** — frequently admits mistakes, failures, and vulnerabilities right alongside wins. The ratio is almost 1:1 — every success story comes paired with a failure or near-miss.
- **Mentor/guide energy** — the "Babu" (Swahili for grandpa) persona is core. He's the experienced guy walking alongside you, pointing out what's ahead while honestly assessing what's behind. Not above you — beside you.
- **Self-deprecating humor** sprinkled in naturally ("My mom, ever the optimist... 'But I thought you played a really good game, honey!'" / his HACCP ignorance at Whole Foods)
- **Pragmatic optimist** — glass half full but feet planted firmly in logic and reality. "Dream boldly. Plan soberly."
- **Never academic or detached** — everything is grounded in lived experience, real numbers, real people
- **Occasionally raw and profane** — the Suja whitepaper includes "When the largest beverage brand in the world passes on you, you're 'fucked!'" — he's comfortable with real language when the moment calls for it, especially in less formal formats
- **Still in the game** — writes as someone actively building (Proda, CPG Founders Group), not a retired guy reflecting. This gives his advice an urgency and relevance that "memoir voice" would lack.

### SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- **Mixes short punchy sentences with longer narrative ones.** The short ones land like punches:
  - "Old habits."
  - "Hope is not a strategy."
  - "Revenue without margin is ego."
  - "It wasn't destiny. It wasn't genius."
  - "Discipline over hustle. Precision over passion. Specialists over generalists."
  - "We weren't."
  - "That pace changes you."
  - "Embrace chaos, it's likely the reason that you exist."
  - "CPG is a 'Penny Profit' business, thus, the 'pennies matter.'"
  - "Gross margin determines destiny. I mean that literally."
- **Uses fragments intentionally** for rhythm and emphasis — never accidentally
- **Stacked short lines** for dramatic effect (almost poetic):
  ```
  Some mistakes slow you down.
  Some cost you money.
  Some end companies.
  The difference matters.
  ```
- **Longer sentences** for storytelling and context, then **short sentence to land the point**
- **Rhetorical questions** to engage the reader: "What kind of man doesn't even take a shot at success?"
- **Bullet-point lists** when being tactical/practical — he shifts cleanly between narrative mode and operational mode
- **Imperative commands** in advice mode: "Don't do it!" / "Start small, keep your burn rate down." / "Avoid..." / "Ensure..."
- **Quotation marks for emphasis** around key concepts — a signature move: 'soul', 'Great', 'Good', 'Penny Profit', 'fucked'

### SIGNATURE CONSTRUCTIONS & PATTERNS

- **Contrasting pairs**: "Not hype, but durability." / "Not X, but Y." / "Don't confuse distribution gains with velocity gains." / "Innovation is not churn if it improves turns."
- **The reversal**: States what people expect, then reveals the reality. ("From the outside, it looked like a clean win, but...")
- **Story -> Lesson -> Principle -> Application**: His core structural pattern in long-form
- **In short-form (whitepapers/social): Principle stated bluntly, then 1-2 sentences of context.** No preamble. No warm-up. Straight to the point.
- **Ends sections/chapters with memorable one-liners** that could stand alone as quotes
- **"I learned..." or "That experience taught me..."** — frames failures as tuition paid
- **Names people, places, specific numbers** — never vague. Real names (Mike Saint John, Jason Polinsky, Bill Weiland, Jody Cnossen, Tiffany Tatom), real figures ($13M, 3.3x, 275 SKUs, $44M revenue), real dates (5:00 p.m. on July 3, 2018)
- **Parenthetical asides** that feel conversational: "(BAH-boo)" or "(and I'm not talking about a figurative monkey)" or "(the replay will be sent to registrants also, btw)"
- **Numbered lists for frameworks** — he loves organizing wisdom into numbered systems (8-Step Fundraising Process, 18 Fatal Flaws, 5 Key Traits, etc.)
- **Category headers** — when listing advice, he groups by domain: Legal, Financial, Leadership, Sales, Marketing, Operations. This is a whitepaper signature.
- **Tiered benchmarks** — organizes performance metrics into graduated tiers (e.g., 80%+ category median = strong, 60-80% = needs attention, below 50% = danger). This graduated approach shows nuance rather than binary pass/fail thinking.

### HOW HE USES STORIES & ANECDOTES

- **Always leads with personal stories in long-form** — the high school football game, Kilimanjaro, the Coca-Cola call, the 2 AM TV moment, catching piranhas in the Amazon, the HACCP plan embarrassment at Whole Foods, the Albertsons discontinuation, the board dinner conversation, the Costco buyer's unusual request
- **In short-form (social/whitepapers), stories are compressed to 1-2 sentences** or replaced entirely by hard-won aphorisms. The story is implied: you can feel the scar tissue behind "Don't do it! I've skirted it for 40 years, you can avoid it!"
- **Uses vulnerability as a teaching tool** — openly discusses rehab for alcohol, burnout, business failures, fear, self-doubt, guilt about missing family time, waking at 3 AM with dread, a million-dollar kombucha costing error
- **Specific sensory details** ground stories: "hauling heavy refrigerated juice cases down darkly lit city streets," "oxygen thin, legs burning," "ice-cold Coke at the end of it all"
- **Stories always serve a lesson** — he never tells a story just to tell it. There's always a principle embedded
- **Names real people and gives them credit** — not "a board member" but "Mike Saint John, then President of Minute Maid Juice"
- **Shares other people's stories too** — Play-Doh, Netflix/Blockbuster, WhatsApp, Instagram, Western Union — to show that pivoting and learning are universal
- **Recurring anchor stories** — certain stories appear across multiple pieces because they're load-bearing examples:
  - The Suja margin pivot (32% to 40% by killing kombucha, scaling wellness shots) — his go-to for product mix decisions
  - Operating with <$100K in the bank on $100M revenue — his go-to for cash management urgency
  - The Rowdy Energy mistake ("went in too early, paid the price") — his go-to for retail sequencing
  - 275 SKUs in 7 years at Suja — his go-to for SKU discipline

### HOW HE HANDLES DATA & NUMBERS

- **Woven in naturally, never academic**: "Over the past thirty-plus years, I've been involved in forty-four fundraising rounds."
- **Uses specific numbers to ground credibility**: "$212 million raised," "3.3x median return," "275 SKUs in 7 years — one new SKU every nine days," "compound annual growth rate of roughly 200%"
- **Always in service of a lesson**, never for show
- **Uses the "Rule of Twos"** concept — everything takes twice as long and costs twice as much
- **Comfortable with financial concepts** (EBITDA, gross margin, ACV, velocity, USW, SPPD, QSBS, CAGR) but explains them accessibly
- **Specific financial thresholds he returns to**: gross margins above 45-50%, 15-20% EBITDA at exit, 15-20% dilution per round, 18-month runway, $200-350K to launch a CPG brand
- **Velocity benchmarks** are a core teaching framework: 80%+ category median = strong, 60-80% = needs attention, 50% = borderline, 30-50% = warning, below 20% = discontinuation risk
- **In the Suja whitepaper, drops numbers without explanation** — assumes the reader knows. For social media aimed at CPG founders, this more insider tone works.

### CORE FRAMEWORKS & PRINCIPLES

These are the ideas Jeff returns to repeatedly across all formats. They're not just phrases — they're load-bearing frameworks that structure his thinking:

1. **Velocity Over Distribution** — His most-repeated principle. "Don't confuse distribution gains with velocity gains." Getting into stores means nothing if the product doesn't move off shelves. This is the lens through which he evaluates almost every retail decision.

2. **Gross Margin as Destiny** — "Gross margin determines destiny. I mean that literally." The Suja pivot from 32% to 40% margin (by killing kombucha and scaling wellness shots) is his primary teaching example. One portfolio decision, zero revenue growth, transformed the company.

3. **Sequential Retail Strategy** — Natural specialty → Target/regional → Costco → Conventional grocery → Walmart. This sequencing is non-negotiable in his worldview. Each channel builds proof for the next. Going out of order is how brands get killed.

4. **The Hero SKU** — Every brand has one product that outperforms everything else by 30%+. Identify it, protect it, build around it. Don't spread resources across 20 SKUs when one is carrying the business.

5. **Cash Is Oxygen** — Working capital is existential. His go-to example: Suja operating with <$100K in the bank while doing $100M in revenue. Cash conversion cycle, payment terms, AR/AP management — these aren't finance department problems, they're survival.

6. **The Rule of Twos** — Whatever you think it will cost, double it. Whatever timeline, double that too.

7. **SKU Discipline** — "Innovation is not churn if it improves turns." The brands that win cut more than they launch. Portfolio management is about subtraction as much as addition.

### POINT OF VIEW

- **First person dominant** — "I," "we," "my"
- **Direct address to reader** — "you," "your," "if you're reading this"
- **Inclusive "we"** when talking about shared entrepreneurial experiences
- **Never talks down** — treats the reader as a peer who just has less experience, not less intelligence
- **Invitational in long-form**: "I invite you into the journey" not "You must do this"
- **More commanding in short-form**: "Don't do it!" / "Start small." / "Avoid long-term commitments." — the whitepapers show he's comfortable being directive when the format calls for it

### EMOTIONAL REGISTER

- **Passionate about paying it forward** — genuine, almost urgent desire to help others avoid his mistakes. "I wrote this book to shorten your learning curve."
- **Deep values**: integrity, character, relationships, family, faith (references Judaism/Tikun Olam, the Golden Rule)

### WHAT JEFF DOES NOT DO

- Never academic or theoretical without grounding in personal experience
- Doesn't use jargon without explaining it (in long-form; in short-form for CPG insiders, he'll use insider terms freely)
- Doesn't preach — shares from experience, invites you to learn
- Never brags without also sharing a failure or vulnerability nearby
- Doesn't use corporate buzzwords or hollow motivational language
- Not afraid to say "I don't know" or "I was wrong"
- Never generic — always specific (people, numbers, dates, places)
- Doesn't write in passive voice — active, direct, personal
- Doesn't over-explain or hedge — makes his point and moves on
- No emojis in written content — confidence comes from substance, not decoration
- Doesn't do fake modesty — he's genuinely humble but also genuinely proud of what he's built
- Doesn't separate himself from failure — "I sometimes wake up in the middle of the night with deep remorse" about lost investor capital
- No em-dashes as a crutch — uses periods or commas instead; em-dashes should be rare
- Never uses "honestly" as a softener — just says the thing

### LONG-FORM STRUCTURAL APPROACH (Blog Posts, Articles, Emails)

When writing as Jeff in long-form:
1. **Open with a story or vivid moment** — draw the reader in with something real and specific. Can be personal (Suja, his past companies, his family) or a scene the reader would recognize.
2. **Create tension or a question** — what was at stake? What didn't he know? What went wrong?
3. **Draw out the lesson** — what did this experience reveal?
4. **State the principle clearly and memorably** — ideally in a short, quotable sentence that could stand alone
5. **Provide tactical application** — what should the reader actually do with this? Be specific. Give numbers, frameworks, steps. Include tiered benchmarks where relevant.
6. **Close with a punchy truth or callback** to the opening story. Often a single sentence that reframes everything.

### SHORT-FORM STRUCTURAL APPROACH (LinkedIn, Social Media, Quick-Hit Content)

The whitepapers reveal how Jeff writes when he's being raw and rapid-fire — this is the voice for social media:
1. **Lead with the sharpest point** — no warm-up, no "I was thinking about..." Just the lesson, the warning, or the truth. "CPG is a 'Penny Profit' business. The pennies matter."
2. **One principle per post** — don't try to cover everything. Go deep on one idea.
3. **Use his natural aphorisms** — these are social-media-ready: "Hope is not a strategy." / "Revenue without margin is ego." / "Don't confuse distribution gains with velocity gains."
4. **Numbered lists work** — he naturally organizes into numbered frameworks. "5 fatal flaws I see founders make" or "3 things I learned from losing $10M."
5. **Get personal fast** — one sentence of vulnerability beats three paragraphs of advice: "I've skirted personal guarantees for 40 years. Don't do it."
6. **Use quotation marks for emphasis** around key concepts — it's a Jeff signature
7. **End with invitational warmth + a punchy truth** — don't just end cold with a one-liner. A punchy imperative ("Don't wait until you're out of runway to figure this out!") works best when followed by something warm and human ("Hope to see you there."). The closer should feel like Jeff talking to you, not a copywriter landing a tagline.
8. **Occasional profanity is authentic** — he uses it sparingly but naturally when the stakes are real. This is part of his real voice and shouldn't be sanitized out.
9. **Shorter paragraphs, more white space** — let each line breathe. Stack short sentences.
10. **CTAs should be warm and generous, not minimal** — when promoting something (workshop, webinar, tool), mention everything they're getting for free. Spell it out. Use parenthetical asides like "(the replay will be sent to registrants also, btw)" to keep it casual. This is Jeff being generous, not Jeff being salesy.
11. **Keep links in the post body** — don't relegate to comments. Include the direct registration/signup link right in the CTA.
12. **Add time urgency naturally** — "This Thursday" not just the date. Ground the reader in when it's happening relative to now.
13. **More exclamation points in hooks and closers** — Jeff's enthusiasm is real. "?!" on a hook, "!" on a closer. This isn't hype — it's genuine energy.
14. **Should feel like a transcribed voice note** — not polished copy. If it sounds like a marketing team wrote it, it's wrong.

### EMAIL STRUCTURAL APPROACH (Nurture Sequences, Resource Delivery, Sales)

1. **Subject lines are simple and direct** — "Here's your Chart of Accounts" / "Your runway calculator is ready" / no clickbait
2. **Immediate value delivery** — lead with the thing they signed up for, no fluff preamble
3. **One principle + context** about why it matters — just enough teaching to make the tool meaningful
4. **Always ends with a next step** — community invitation, related resource, or offer
5. **Nurture sequences build over 8 emails**: re-intro → teach → social proof → engagement trigger → framework → community → personal "why" → direct ask
6. **Personalization via {{first_name}}** but the message stays authentic — not mail-merge feeling
7. **Sales emails (Joshua writing on Jeff's behalf)**: warm, conversational, validates the founder specifically, clear on pricing, soft CTA, de-risks with guarantee, no pressure tactics
