# Jeff Church — Brand Ecosystem Design Brief

**One ridgeline, four brands.**

Jeff Church's world currently reads as four unrelated things. This brief defines how they relate, and the single visual idea that can hold them together.

*Design brief · Brand ecosystem · v1 · August 2026*

---

## The idea: a summit ridgeline and a growth curve are the same shape

A single rising line with peaks and drawdowns is a mountain range to a climber and a revenue chart to a founder. Climb, peak, the drop, the recovery, a higher summit.

It is also literally Jeff's story: zero to $100M in six years, the July 3rd call when Coca-Cola walked away, the turnaround, and Suja ringing the Nasdaq bell at north of $700M without him in the room.

That double reading is the asset. It's specific to this man, it can't be borrowed by a competitor, and it flexes — every brand in the system takes a different segment of the same line.

**Please treat this as a starting provocation, not a finished mark.** We want your interpretation of it, and we want to see what else you find. If you land on something stronger that abandons the idea entirely, show us that.

> **What we're replacing.** The current CPG Founders Group mark is a three-peak mountain with a summit flag that reads as stock clipart. The metaphor is right and worth keeping — Jeff has climbed six of the Seven Summits, "Babu" was earned on Kilimanjaro, and the book's final chapter is entirely about the climb. The execution needs to be rebuilt from scratch.

---

## Architecture: how the pieces relate

Four entities, three tiers. Dreammakers sits on top and may stay largely internal — the holding company and the philosophy rather than a loud consumer-facing brand. Everything a founder actually buys sits one level down.

### Umbrella — Dreammakers

The holding entity and the philosophy. Exists to help entrepreneurs change their stars — but the emphasis belongs on **makers**, not dreamers. May remain internal or investor-facing; it does not need to fight for attention with the brands below it.

### The signature — Jeff Church

The person. Founder of Dreammakers, author of the book, the face of the advisory, the name on the cap table. Not a logo — a signature, a photograph, and a byline. Appears across everything without competing with it.

### Arm · people — CPG Founders Group

The coaching and advisory arm, plus investments into brands on breakout trajectories. Serves the next generation of CPG founders building the next big consumer brand.

- MBA for CPG — self-paced online course
- Free resources — calculators, readiness test, webinars
- Private advisory & consulting
- The Founders Club — free community, 300+ founders
- Investments

### Arm · software — Babu

An AI chat platform and knowledge base built specifically for CPG founders. Always on, self-serve, 50+ specialized advisors.

**Naming convention (settled):** the brand is **Babu**, or **Babu AI** where it needs disambiguating from the persona in the book. `askbabu.ai` is the domain only — it is a URL, not the brand name, and should not appear in the logo lockup.

### Arm · the book — The Cold-Pressed Truth

*A Founder's Field Guide to Building, Scaling, and Selling a Consumer Brand.* By Jeff Church, foreword by Jay Shetty. The credibility anchor and the top of the funnel for everything above.

## The organizing principle: warm where it's human, cool where it's a machine

The hardest job in this system is making CPG Founders Group and Babu obviously related but obviously different — a founder landing on both should immediately understand which one they need.

The cleanest line is **people versus software**. One is Jeff, a cohort, a room, capital. The other is always-on, self-serve, 3 a.m.

Let temperature carry that difference. Same palette, same mark system, same type — but the human side runs warm and the software side runs cool. That gives you real differentiation without four unrelated identities.

| Brand | Temperature | What it is | Serves | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dreammakers** | Warm · quiet | The parent. Restrained, confident, almost corporate. Should feel like the entity that *owns* things, not the entity that *sells* things. | Jeff, partners, LPs, internal | Dream boldly. Plan soberly. |
| **CPG Founders Group** | Warm | The coaching and capital arm. Human, earned, in-the-trenches. The handshake and the room full of founders. | CPG founders, pre-launch to exit | The operator who's done it eight times |
| **Babu** | Cool | The software. Precise, fast, instrument-like. Should feel like a tool a serious operator keeps open in a tab, not a chatbot. | Same founders, self-serve, 24/7 | Answers, not encouragement |
| **The Cold-Pressed Truth** | Warm · editorial | The book. The most editorial artifact in the system and the one that sets the tone for everything else. | Founders, press, speaking bookers | Anti-hype. Admits what it cost. |

## The mark: one system, four readings

Rough provocations only — these explain the thinking, they are not to be executed.

| Brand | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Dreammakers** | The full range. The whole arc, no single peak emphasised. |
| **CPG Founders Group** | One peak, one flag. The summit being climbed right now. |
| **Babu** | The same ridge, resolved into data. Mountain becomes chart. |
| **The book** | The honest curve, drawdown marked. The chart nobody shows you. |

### Requirements for the final mark

- Works at 16px as a favicon **and** at 40 feet on a conference backdrop
- Survives one colour, reversed, and embossed
- Does not require the wordmark to be legible
- **Drawn, not assembled from stock geometry** — the current logo's biggest failure is that it looks bought

## Colour: mountain at dawn

Jeff wants to carry the blue from Babu across the ecosystem, and we agree — with one adjustment. The current Babu blue is an electric blue with a violet gradient, which is the house style of nearly every AI product shipped in the last two years. It will date quickly and it's the least distinctive thing on the page.

**Deepen the blue, keep the gold.** Navy as the ground, one bright blue held back for signal and interface, gold as the warm accent.

Blue and gold is cold rock and warm summit light — it ties the palette to the mountain to the story. The gold also carries real meaning: it's the colour of the two-ounce turmeric shot that ran roughly 60% gross margin against 12% on the kombucha it replaced, and quietly saved Suja.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Summit Navy** | `#0B1A2E` | Primary ground |
| **Ridge Blue** | `#1B3A5F` | Secondary surfaces |
| **Signal Blue** | `#2B4CF0` | From Babu. Interface only. |
| **Summit Gold** | `#DFA13C` | The warm accent |
| **Deep Amber** | `#A56A16` | Warm side, print |
| **Paper** | `#F7F5F1` | Warm off-white |
| **Stone** | `#75828F` | Muted text |

These are a proposed starting point, not a locked palette — push them. The one firm constraint is the relationship: **cool ground, warm accent, no third hue.** And no violet gradients.

## Typography: one pairing, split by temperature

A single type system across all four brands, with the ratio shifting by context. The human and editorial side leans on the serif; the software side leans on the grotesque. Same fonts, different emphasis — that's what makes them feel related without feeling identical.

| Role | Direction | Where it leads |
|---|---|---|
| **Display** | A high-contrast serif with some edge. Canela, Tiempos Headline, GT Sectra, or Playfair if budget is a factor. | Book · Dreammakers · CPGFG |
| **Text & UI** | A neutral grotesque with a real range of weights. Söhne, Founders Grotesk, National 2, or Inter. | Babu · all body copy |
| **Data & labels** | A mono for metrics, eyebrows, and anything instrument-like. | Dashboards · field-guide products |

Note that Babu currently runs Inter throughout. If we license a grotesque, it needs a webfont licence and a variable-weight file.

## Guardrails: the tone this has to hold

The book is the tonal centre of gravity for the whole ecosystem, and it's deliberately anti-hype. It opens on a man weeping on July 3rd after Coca-Cola walked away, and includes a chapter on burnout, drinking, and a month in rehab. That honesty **is** the brand's differentiation in a category full of growth-hack promises.

**Lean into**

- Earned, operator-grade, unglamorous competence
- Instruments and field guides — things you actually use
- Restraint. One accent doing the work.
- Warmth without softness. Babu is a grandfather, not a mascot.
- Real numbers and real charts as ornament

**Avoid**

- "Unfair advantage," "10x," "secrets," "hacks"
- Violet-to-blue AI gradients and glowing orbs
- Hustle imagery, hockey-stick charts, rocket ships
- Literal juice, fruit, or droplets — Suja is his past, not his brand
- Twee outdoorsiness: compasses, topo textures, leather, campfires

## Still being decided on our side

1. **How public is Dreammakers?** Fully internal holding entity, investor-facing, or a real consumer-facing brand? This changes how much identity it needs. Our current lean is internal-to-investor-facing.

2. **Does the investment arm get its own name?** CPG Founders Group currently holds both coaching and investing — two audiences, two sales motions. It may want to split later. Please don't design it in, but don't design it out either.

3. **Does Babu stay a wordmark or gain a character?** Babu is Swahili for grandpa — a name the Kilimanjaro guides gave Jeff for his slow pace and unsolicited trail advice. There's a real character in there. We're wary of a cartoon mascot but open to something more restrained.

4. **Rebuild or rebrand?** cpgfoundersgroup.com is live with members and SEO. We're not renaming it. How far the visual overhaul goes is a budget question we should discuss.

## From the book

These are Jeff's own words from the manuscript. They're included because **the mountain is not a metaphor we invented — it's autobiography**, and the design will be better if you can hear his voice before you draw anything. Each group is evidence for a specific decision in this brief.

### Why the mountain is real, and not a stock metaphor

From Chapter 23, *The Journey & The Destination*:

> "More than 30 years ago, while I was flying into Seattle late one night on business, a bright full moon illuminated the 14,411-foot summit of Mt Rainier. The sight of the mountain's shimmering silhouette through the airplane window captivated me. It was so magnificent, I couldn't help but stare! Right then and there, I promised myself that I would stand on that summit one day. Less than a year later, I kept that promise."

> "That first climb changed me. It birthed in me a passion that eventually led me to all seven continents of the world. I've since climbed six of the most magnificent mountains on the planet, and have stood on five of those mountains' summits."

> "As I write these words, I am enroute from San Diego to Kathmandu to tackle another climbing dream and check another mountain off my bucket list. The locals refer to it as 'Chomolungma' or 'the Goddess Mother of the World.' We refer to it as the tallest mountain in the world: Mt Everest."

He does the same thing with the Amazon — sees it from a plane window, and goes:

> "In less than a year, there I was, standing on the banks of the Amazon, catching piranhas in the dark. The detail I hadn't factored in was the monkey on my back. And I'm not talking about a figurative monkey. But at least he seemed friendly."

**Why this matters for the design:** he is not a businessman who likes mountain imagery. He is a mountaineer who runs businesses. The mark should feel earned, technical, and slightly weathered — not aspirational stock summitry.

### Who Babu actually is

From the Preface:

> "A few years ago, while climbing Kilimanjaro with my kids, around day three, with oxygen thin, legs burning, the local guides started calling me 'Babu' (BAH-boo), which is Swahili for Grandpa. I earned the title partly because of my slow pace, and partly because I kept offering unsolicited advice on the trail.
>
> Old habits.
>
> When you spend as much time as I have at altitude, you develop opinions, along with a collection of tricks, hacks, and fatal flaws to avoid.
>
> The name stuck. My grandkids even call me Babu now.
>
> Somewhere between the mountain and the boardroom, I realized that's exactly the role I've always played: **the experienced guy walking alongside you, pointing out what lies ahead while honestly assessing the truth of what's behind.**"

And the closing restatement, from Chapter 23:

> "And in many ways, the role of 'Babu' that my kids gave me so many years ago, is the one I've tried to play for you throughout this book. **Someone who walks ahead, pointing out the cliffs, the switchbacks, and the places where the view is worth the climb.**"

**Why this matters for the design:** Babu is *beside you on the trail*, not above you on a stage. That's the difference between a guide and a guru — and it's the argument against both a cartoon mascot and a hero-founder portrait. It also means the software should feel like a companion, not an oracle.

### The tone the whole system has to survive

The book is relentlessly anti-hype, and that honesty is the differentiation. From the Preface:

> "If the other books on your shelf only talk about grand visions and seamless execution, they are not adequately preparing you for what it truly takes."

> "Starting and scaling a business, especially in the hyper-competitive CPG market, is messy, exhausting, and unforgiving. I remember the confusion, the isolation, and the paralyzing dread that came from not knowing which proverbial play to run next. I searched for a definitive guide born from the real pain of building a CPG brand from nothing. **I never found one, so I wrote it.**"

From Chapter 16, on the July 3rd call when Coca-Cola declined to buy the rest of Suja:

> "When I hung up the phone and walked downstairs, I lost my composure in front of my boys and started weeping. I wasn't thinking about awards or growth percentages at that moment. I was wondering, 'How did I let us get to this place?'"

From Chapter 19, on what it cost:

> "Ironically, while running a 'better-for-you' beverage company, I neglected my own health. After working eighty-hour weeks for nearly eight years at Suja, I was completely burned out. I was drinking far too much alcohol. In fact, after leaving the company, I spent a month in rehab to reset my life and health."

**Why this matters for the design:** nothing in this identity can promise an outcome. No hockey sticks, no "unfair advantage," no glowing gradients. The visual system should feel like it has been through something.

### The climb-versus-summit argument — the ridgeline concept in his words

From Chapter 23:

> "I could never stand on a summit without the long, challenging journey that would inevitably test my endurance, my strength, and my resilience. And as breathtaking as every summit has been, the steps it took to get there shaped me and built my stamina."

> "I wouldn't dream of fishing in piranha-infested waters or climbing to breathtaking summits without guides, proper tools, careful planning, and contingency plans for inclement weather."

> "**You don't wait until you feel ready to start the climb.**"

> "**Both in business and in life, the summit may be the goal, but the real transformation happens on the climb up.** So raise your hand. Take the step. Start the climb."

And the same idea applied to retail, from Chapter 12 — proof the metaphor already runs through the business content, not just the memoir:

> "Imagine being a champion climber, clawing your way up a mountain, only to realize that reaching the summit was merely half the battle; staying there was a whole new and equally formidable challenge. Once on top, you meet others who have also brought their A game, hoping to knock you off the top."

**Why this matters for the design:** this is the argument for the ridgeline over a single triangle. A lone peak says *arrival*. A ridge with drawdowns says *the climb continues, and it costs you* — which is the book's actual thesis and the more honest mark.

### The umbrella philosophy — where "Dreammakers" comes from and how to read it

From the Preface, where Jeff uses the phrase himself:

> "…engineer selling your startup and in doing so **change your and your loved one's stars.**"

And the four words from Chapter 2 that should govern how Dreammakers is expressed:

> "Most founders are wired as 'glass-half-full' optimists. I'm one of them. But I've learned that the healthiest entrepreneurs can keep their heads in the clouds while also planting their feet firmly on the ground in logic and reality.
>
> **Dream boldly. Plan soberly.**"

**Why this matters for the design:** the emphasis in *Dreammakers* belongs on the second half. The dream is the easy part; the making is the job. Expressed that way, the umbrella stops competing with the book and starts agreeing with it.

### The line the whole system should pass

From the Epilogue, written after Suja rang the Nasdaq opening bell in May 2026 at a valuation north of $700M — and after the current owners declined Jeff's request to attend:

> "Ringing that bell had been a lifelong dream of mine. I wasn't there to ring it."

> "The bell is a moment. The work is the point. The people you build with are the point. The decisions you make on Tuesdays when nobody is watching are the point. If you do those things well for long enough, sometimes a bell rings. Sometimes it doesn't. **Both outcomes are honorable if the work was honest.**"

> "**Build the company, but don't be the company. Love the work, but don't be the work.**"

**Why this matters for the design:** this is why Jeff Church is the signature and not the umbrella. The identity has to be able to outlive him.
