# Weekly letter

Not a link roundup. One idea, told well, one question, one rotating PS. Working name: **"The Tuesday Letter."**

## Structure

1. **Subject:** lowercase, specific, sounds like a note from a person. Pull the hook from that week's best-performing LinkedIn post.
2. **The idea (150-300 words):** the LinkedIn post, expanded for email. Story first, then the lesson, then the one-liner. Jeff's voice.
3. **Founder Mailbag (50-150 words):** one real question from a reply, an application, or the WhatsApp group (anonymized, permission asked if it's identifiable), and Jeff's straight answer. Skip this section the weeks there isn't a good one.
4. **Question:** one line asking for a reply. "What would you do?" / "Where's your number?"
5. **PS (rotates weekly):**
   - Week 1: Strategy Session
   - Week 2: Free resource or event
   - Week 3: MBA for CPG
   - Week 4: Babu

Every PS link gets the link-click → `Hot Lead` automation.

## Sample issue

**Subject:** what's a HACCP plan?

Hey {{first_name}},

When we launched Suja I'd already started or bought six companies. I had a Harvard MBA. I'd been a CPA. I figured I knew how to run a business.

Then a Whole Foods buyer asked me, "Do you have a HACCP plan?"

I said, out loud, "What's a HACCP plan?" I figured it was a one-page form.

It is not a one-page form. A week later I was sitting in an all-day food safety course learning how many ways a beverage company can accidentally hurt people.

Ignorance is expensive in any business. In CPG it's especially expensive, because the industry has its own language, its own math and its own rules, and nobody hands you the rulebook.

The fix isn't knowing everything. It's knowing what you don't know before the buyer does. Before every big meeting, ask yourself: what's the question I'd least like to be asked? Then go get the answer.

**From the mailbag**

*"We just got a meeting with a regional chain. What do they actually care about?"*

Velocity, margin for them, and whether you'll support the product once it's on shelf. They're not buying your story. They're buying proof it'll turn. Bring your sales per store per week from wherever you're selling now, your promo plan for the first 90 days, and a clear answer on who handles demos.

What's the question you'd least like a buyer to ask you? Hit reply. I might answer it next week.

Talk soon,
Jeff
